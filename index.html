Two filings, one engine: Maruti Suzuki vs. Mahindra & Mahindra, FY24

A peer comparison between two real FY24 consolidated filings in the same sector, with five years of history behind each figure. Maruti Suzuki is a focused passenger-vehicle manufacturer; Mahindra & Mahindra's consolidated numbers also carry its financial-services subsidiary, which shows up clearly in the leverage read below.

Maruti Suzuki India Ltd · consolidated · year ended Mar 31, 2024 · ₹ crore Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd · consolidated · year ended Mar 31, 2024 · ₹ crore
The read: both automakers clawed back from pandemic-era margin compression, but along different paths. Maruti's net margin nearly doubled from FY22's low of 4.4% to 9.5% in FY24, and its ROE rebounded to 15.8% on a balance sheet that's still almost debt-free. M&M's turnaround is sharper — from a FY20 loss to a FY23 peak ROE of 20.2% — but its FY24 return leans more on leverage (a 3.57× equity multiplier, largely the financial-services book) than on margin alone. Under a uniform stress scenario, Maruti's near-zero debt gives it far more room to absorb a bad year: interest coverage stays above 80× even after the shock, while M&M's falls toward 2.8×.

Methodology & caveats

  1. Figures are the actual consolidated FY24 (and, for the trend section, FY20–FY24) balance sheet and P&L line items as filed by each company — pulled directly from their published financials, not estimated.
  2. Mahindra & Mahindra's consolidated filing folds in Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services, an NBFC lending arm, plus minority interests in partly-owned subsidiaries. Every ROE and equity-multiplier figure here uses equity attributable to M&M's own shareholders (excluding minority interest), matching net profit which is also reported on that basis — using the fully consolidated equity figure instead would understate ROE by roughly 3-4 points. Current assets, current liabilities, and total debt still reflect the full consolidated group, which is why they look unusually large next to a "pure" auto manufacturer like Maruti.
  3. "COGS" is approximated as total reported operating expenses for both companies (neither publishes a clean, directly comparable cost-of-materials-only line at the consolidated level), so gross and operating margin read identically here — treat them as one combined profitability read rather than two distinct figures.
  4. Peer-relative anomaly detection (z-scores) is intentionally not shown — it requires 3 or more companies to be statistically meaningful and silently misfires with just two. A bank-specific mode (NPA ratio, CASA ratio, capital adequacy) is also out of scope here — this schema assumes a manufacturing/services cost structure that doesn't map onto a bank's balance sheet.
  5. This is a mechanics demonstration, not investment research. No forward-looking claims are made about either company.
